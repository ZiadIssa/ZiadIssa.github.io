Problems I've solved.

Real problems from real clients, and what I did about them.

Online store

Order emails that couldn't keep up

Problem

The email service capped out at 200 emails a month, about 100 orders, and its keys were sitting in the site's public code.

Fix

Moved order emails into the database itself: 300 a day for free, keys locked away, designed in the store's colours with product photos.

Online store

Invisible on Google

Problem

Products showed up with no picture or price, hundreds of pages weren't indexed, and old pages from a previous site still ranked.

Fix

Rewrote every title and description, added product data with price and stock, built a sitemap from the database and server-rendered the shop.

Security

One password, properly locked

Problem

A team of three wanted one shared admin login, not separate accounts, but the database behind it was wide open.

Fix

Locked every table, hashed the password and routed every admin change through checks in the database. No extra logins, nothing exposed.

Point of sale

A till that never loses a sale

Problem

A shop counter can't stop when the internet drops or the power cuts in the middle of a sale.

Fix

The POS runs fully offline and saves every change safely, so a crash can't corrupt the data. End-of-day cash counts show over or short at a glance.

HR system

Check-ins nobody can fake

Problem

Staff could check in for each other, or check in without being at the shop.

Fix

Checking in needs the worker's own registered phone, a location inside the shop and a QR code at the counter that keeps changing.

Cake shop

Orders that fit the kitchen

Problem

Every cake is made to order, with flavours, fillings, custom flower colours and prep times of three to five hours or more.

Fix

An order flow that prices every choice as you pick it, and only offers same-day delivery when the kitchen can actually make it.

How a song becomes a level.

A C++ engine inside the game listens to your track and writes the chart in seconds.

Beatmap generator
Tempo124 BPM

WaveformOnset strengthVocal energyChartIllustration with a sample signal
  1. 01DecodeMP3 and OGG files are decoded natively in C++, so even long tracks load in moments.
  2. 02DetectThe engine finds onsets, the instants a new sound starts, and locks them to the tempo.
  3. 03Follow the voiceEnergy in the vocal range decides which onsets become notes, so the chart sings along.
  4. 04ShapeShort hits become taps, sustained notes and shreds become holds, and difficulty sets the density.

Case study

Taking a 30-year-old store online.

Aladdin Video Games has sold games and electronics for more than 30 years. I built everything they run on now: the online store, the admin panel behind it, and the software at the counter.

30+years in business
4systems built
2languages, Arabic and English
3people run all of it
The starting point

A great store with no way to sell online.

Aladdin is run by a team of three, so whatever I built had to run without anyone babysitting it. Their domain still pointed at an unfinished WordPress site, and its old pages were still the ones showing up on Google.

They needed a store customers could actually buy from, a back office they could manage themselves, and tools for the counter that keep working when the internet doesn't.

What I built

Four systems, one store.

Online store

  • A PC builder for putting together a custom rig
  • Mega-menu, spec filters and search in both languages
  • Dark mode, recently viewed items and install-as-app

Admin panel

  • Products, offers and orders in one place
  • A menus editor that drives the whole navbar
  • Full backups with a selective restore

Desktop ERP

  • Installment sales for bigger purchases
  • A cash drawer counter and scheduled backups
  • Shipped with a full PDF user manual

Point of sale

  • Barcode scanning, discounts and printed receipts
  • End-of-day cash counts and Z-reports
  • Works fully offline, and a crash can't corrupt a sale
Going live

Launch was more than flipping a switch.

  1. 01

    Their own domain

    Moved aladdinvideogames.com onto Cloudflare without breaking the mailboxes they already used.

  2. 02

    Order emails that scale

    Replaced a 200-a-month email service with emails sent from the database: 300 a day for free, in the store's own colours, with product photos.

  3. 03

    Locked down

    Every table locked, the admin password hashed, and every admin change checked by the database itself.

  4. 04

    Found on Google

    New titles, product data with price and stock, a sitemap built from the database and server-rendered shop pages, to replace the old WordPress results.

Since launch

They keep coming back for more.

After launch, Aladdin started paying for new features: a menus tab so they can rebuild the navbar themselves, a three-level category menu with images, and a dark mode that keeps their white-background product photos looking right.

And because it's a games store, there's a playable Pac-Man hidden in the navbar, with a maze shaped like the word ALADDIN.
